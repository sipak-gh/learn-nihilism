# Appendix B — Timeline

The history of nihilism from its ancient intimations to today, with the chapter that covers each event. Ancient dates are approximate, and several are disputed among scholars; works are dated by first publication unless noted. Previous: [Appendix A — Glossary](appendix-a-glossary.md). Next: [Appendix C — Reading List](appendix-c-reading-list.md).

## Ancient and medieval

| Date | Event | Chapter |
|---|---|---|
| 6th–5th c. BCE | Theognis and, later, Sophocles (*Oedipus at Colonus*) voice the "wisdom of Silenus": best never to be born, next best to die soon | [2](02-ancient-roots.md) |
| 5th c. BCE | Gorgias, *On Not-Being*: nothing exists; if it did, it could not be known; if known, it could not be communicated | [2](02-ancient-roots.md) |
| c. 334–324 BCE | Pyrrho of Elis travels with Alexander the Great's expedition to India | [2](02-ancient-roots.md) |
| c. 3rd c. BCE (disputed) | Ecclesiastes (*Qoheleth*): "vanity of vanities; all is vanity" | [1](01-what-is-nihilism.md), [2](02-ancient-roots.md) |
| c. 300 BCE | The Cyrenaic Hegesias, the "death-persuader," argues that happiness is impossible | [2](02-ancient-roots.md) |
| c. 55 BCE | Lucretius, *On the Nature of Things*: an atomic universe without purpose, and "death is nothing to us" | [2](02-ancient-roots.md) |
| c. 2nd c. CE | Nāgārjuna, *Fundamental Verses on the Middle Way*: all things are empty (*śūnyatā*), and emptiness is not nihilism | [2](02-ancient-roots.md) |
| c. 2nd–3rd c. CE | Sextus Empiricus, *Outlines of Pyrrhonism*; Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* (c. 170–180) | [2](02-ancient-roots.md), [15](15-beyond-nihilism.md) |
| c. 3rd c. CE | Diogenes Laertius, *Lives of the Eminent Philosophers*, preserves the stories of Pyrrho and the Cyrenaics | [2](02-ancient-roots.md) |
